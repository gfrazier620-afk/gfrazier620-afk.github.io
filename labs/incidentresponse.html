Incident Response: Working a Compromised Account

A simulated incident walked through the NIST SP 800-61 lifecycle: an external IP brute-forces a server, logs in, and creates a backdoor admin account. The goal was to practice the order of operations and the documentation, not just the commands.

Walking the NIST phases

  1. Preparation

    Confirm the tooling and access are ready: log retention, an IR contact list, admin access to the host, and a place to record notes and evidence.

  2. Detection & analysis

    Validate it's a true positive (the success from the attacking IP confirms it), then scope it: which accounts, which hosts, and has the IP touched anything else? Assign severity: High, because there's confirmed access plus persistence.

  3. Containment

    Stop the bleeding first, while keeping evidence intact:

    Disable-LocalUser -Name "svc_backup"
Disable-LocalUser -Name "helpdesk2"
New-NetFirewallRule -DisplayName "IR-001 block attacker" `
    -Direction Inbound -RemoteAddress 203.0.113.45 -Action Block
  4. Eradication

    Remove the persistence and the way in: delete the rogue account, reset svc_backup's password, and check for scheduled tasks, services, or Run keys created during the window.

    Remove-LocalUser -Name "helpdesk2"
Get-ScheduledTask | Where-Object { $_.Date -and [datetime]$_.Date -gt (Get-Date).AddDays(-1) }
  5. Recovery

    Re-enable service accounts with new credentials, watch the host closely for 48 hours, and confirm no new 4720 or 4732 events appear.

  6. Post-incident activity

    Write the report: summary, timeline, impact, actions taken, and recommendations. Here they were an account lockout policy, no network logon for service accounts, MFA on remote access, and an alert for 4720 followed by 4732.

Key takeaways

  • Contain before you eradicate. Deleting the rogue account first destroys evidence and tips off the attacker.
  • The timeline is the backbone of the report. Build it as you go, not after.
  • Every incident should leave behind at least one new detection or control.
